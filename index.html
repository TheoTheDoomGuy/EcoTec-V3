Low-carbon home heating

Air source heat pumps

An air source heat pump is a highly efficient, low-carbon heating system that extracts heat from the outside air and uses it to warm your home and hot water. Even in cold weather, it can pull usable heat from the air, making it one of the most energy-efficient heating technologies available today.

Heat pumps deliver more heat energy than the electricity they consume, making them significantly more efficient than traditional boilers — a smart, future-proof investment.

Common questions

Will a heat pump work in cold weather?

Modern heat pumps can operate in cold weather, including temperatures around -15°C. Performance depends on the model, system design and installation.

Do I need underfloor heating?

Not always. Heat pumps can work with radiators, underfloor heating or a combination of both.

  • High seasonal efficiency

    Many modern heat pumps achieve a SCOP of 3–4: three to four units of heat for every unit of electricity used.

  • Lower running costs

    Using ambient air rather than burning fuel can reduce heating bills, especially in well-insulated homes. Actual costs depend on the system, home and energy tariffs.

  • Consistent, steady heat

    Runs continuously at low power, avoiding the peaks and troughs of gas boilers.

  • Ideal for renewable integration

    Paired with solar PV, it can run on clean, self-generated electricity.

  • Reduced carbon emissions

    No combustion and far lower energy use help cut household carbon output.

  • Support long after installation

    Most installers fit and move on. We make sure your system is set up, maintained and optimised for real-world performance.

Solar power

Generate clean electricity and reduce your energy bills. We design and install high-performance solar photovoltaic (PV) systems for homes and businesses.

Systems can include solar panels, inverters and battery storage, with aftercare available on request.

  • Lower energy bills

    Many customers see a 40–70% reduction in their energy bills, especially when paired with battery storage. Actual savings vary with system size, energy use and tariffs.

  • Battery storage

    Store the power you generate for maximum energy independence.

  • Roof or ground mounted

    Installations designed around your property, on the roof or on the ground.

  • Works all year round

    Panels still generate on cloudy days, and the South West performs well year-round.

  • Usually no planning permission

    Solar normally falls under permitted development, unless your home is listed or in a conservation area.

  • Quick installation

    Most installations take 2–5 days, depending on the size of the array and the roof.

Charging at home

EV charging

A dedicated charging unit installed on an exterior wall or garage gives you convenient charging at home, without relying on public chargers.

Most UK homes can have a charger fitted if they have a modern consumer unit (fuseboard) and spare capacity on the incoming electrical supply.

  • Faster charging

    A typical 7 kW charger is much faster than a standard three-pin socket.

  • Convenient home charging

    Charge on your own driveway or garage wall, whenever suits you.

  • Smart features

    Smart chargers use Wi-Fi or 4G for monitoring and smart charging.

  • Works with all EVs

    We install universal chargers suitable for all electric and plug-in hybrid vehicles.

  • Quick installation

    Most installations take 1–2 days, depending on the consumer unit and cable routing.

  • Suitability check

    We check your consumer unit and supply first. Older units may need a small upgrade.

External wall insulation

See where heat escapes

Thermal imaging surveys

A thermal imaging survey shows exactly where your home is losing heat and where improvements could make a difference.

It’s a simple first step before investing in insulation, a heat pump or other upgrades.

  • Cold spots and draughts

    Pinpoint the areas of your home that feel cold or draughty.

  • Missing insulation

    Find gaps where insulation is missing or has settled.

  • Damp or moisture issues

    Spot moisture problems before they get worse.

  • Heat loss

    See heat escaping through walls, windows and roofs.

  • Air leakage and cold bridging

    Identify leaks and weak points in the building fabric.

  • Honest next steps

    Straightforward advice on the improvements that will make the biggest difference.
